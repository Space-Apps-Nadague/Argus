## Career Dose Limits & ALARA Shielding Principles

NASA Office of the Chief Health and Medical Officer (OCHMO) sets a universal career effective radiation dose limit of 600 mSv across all astronauts, paired with As Low As Reasonably Achievable (ALARA) operational management:
- Deep-space galactic cosmic rays (GCR) and acute solar energetic protons require continuous time-resolved tracking (nominal 0g transit 0.4–1.2 mSv/d; yellow caution 1.2–2.5 mSv/d; red alert >2.5 mSv/d).
- During elevated flux, maximize polyethylene, water-storage, and food-locker mass shielding around the crew sleep stations and storm shelter, and postpone all non-essential extravehicular activity (EVA).
- Log cumulative mission dose and 24-hour dose rate before and after every shelter interval.

## Hematopoietic & Immune Suppression Clinical Protocol

Ionizing space radiation preferentially suppresses radiosensitive bone marrow progenitors and circulating lymphocytes (`wbc_lymph`, nominal 1.5–3.5 k/uL; caution 1.2–1.5 k/uL; alert <1.2 k/uL):
1. Perform a point-of-care capillary finger-prick Complete Blood Count (CBC) to quantify absolute lymphocyte count (`wbc_lymph`) at baseline, 6 hours, and 24 hours after any elevated radiation exposure.
2. Monitor core body temperature (`core_temp`) for fever (>38.0 C) and inspect skin barriers for radiation erythema or opportunistic dermatitis.
3. Administer oral electrolyte hydration (500 mL every 2 hours), antioxidant micronutrient packs (Vitamins C, E, and selenium), and prophylactic antiemetics if radiation prodromal nausea occurs.
4. Enforce strict contact hygiene and HEPA air filtration if absolute lymphocyte count drops below 1.2 k/uL, and transmit serial CBC trends to the Earth Flight Surgeon.
