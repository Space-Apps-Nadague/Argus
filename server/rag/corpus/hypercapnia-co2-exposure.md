## Inspired ppCO2 Thresholds & Symptom Recognition

Closed-loop spacecraft habitats accumulate localized carbon dioxide pockets in microgravity due to absent natural convection. Per NASA-STD-3001 Volume 1 crew health standards and ARGUS operational bands:
- Nominal 0g / orbital inspired ppCO2: 1.2–3.5 mmHg (keep average inspired ppCO2 as low as operationally achievable to prevent cerebral vasodilation and cognitive performance decrements).
- Caution band (3.5–4.5 mmHg): frontal throbbing headache upon waking, mild tachypnea, reduced sleep efficiency, and increased Psychomotor Vigilance Test (PVT) lapses.
- Alert band (>4.5 mmHg): acute hypercapnia, air hunger, dyspnea on exertion, tachycardia, visual disturbance, and impaired operational decision-making.

## Hypercapnia Clinical & Environmental Mitigation Steps

Execute this complete clinical unit whenever a crew member reports a frontal CO2 headache, stuffy air, or cabin ppCO2 exceeds 3.5 mmHg:
1. Move the affected astronaut immediately to a high-airflow duct outlet and position a personal ventilation fan directed across the breathing zone to disperse stagnant boundary-layer CO2.
2. Inspect the Carbon Dioxide Removal Assembly (CDRA) desiccant/adsorbent beds or switch to backup Lithium Hydroxide (LiOH) scrubber canisters.
3. Suspend high-output aerobic and ARED resistance exercise for all crew until cabin ppCO2 drops below 3.5 mmHg.
4. If ppCO2 exceeds 4.5 mmHg or air hunger/confusion is present, place the crew member on a portable breathing apparatus (100% O2 mask at 6 L/min), check SpO2 and resting heart rate, and queue an immediate priority telemetry packet to Earth Mission Control.
