## Optic Nerve Sheath Ultrasound & Tonometry Self-Exam

Cephalad fluid shift in microgravity and partial gravity elevates intracranial and orbital venous pressure, producing optic disc edema, globe flattening, choroidal folds, and hyperopic visual shift (Spaceflight-Associated Neuro-Ocular Syndrome, SANS). Perform this clinical unit whenever near vision blurs, retro-orbital headache develops, or Optic Nerve Sheath Diameter (ONSD) exceeds 5.5 mm:
1. Seat or restrain the crew member with head neutral and eyelids closed; apply sterile ophthalmic ultrasound gel over the upper eyelid.
2. Place the high-frequency linear ultrasound probe in the transverse plane and measure ONSD 3 mm posterior to the globe in both eyes (0g nominal 4.5–5.5 mm; yellow caution >5.5 mm; red alert >5.9 mm).
3. Measure seated intraocular pressure (IOP) with the handheld rebound tonometer, averaging 3 readings per eye (0g nominal 11–18 mmHg; red alert >21 mmHg).
4. Complete a monocular Amsler grid and near-vision acuity check at 40 cm to screen for central scotoma or metamorphopsia, and capture non-mydriatic retinal fundus images for Earth Flight Surgeon downlink.

## Cranial Venous Unloading & LBNP Countermeasures

When ONSD or IOP drifts into the Caution or Alert band, initiate non-pharmacological venous fluid redistribution steps immediately:
1. Conduct a 45-minute Lower Body Negative Pressure (LBNP / Chibis) session at -25 mmHg or don thigh venous occlusion cuffs per onboard schedule to draw fluid volume caudally.
2. Inspect and switch to the updated +0.75D to +1.50D adjustable spaceflight reading spectacles for close-up procedure panels.
3. Verify habitat inspired ppCO2 is below 3.0 mmHg, as elevated CO2 causes cerebral vasodilation and compounds optic nerve sheath distension.
4. Avoid heavy Valsalva straining during resistance exercise until repeat ONSD ultrasound confirms stabilization.
